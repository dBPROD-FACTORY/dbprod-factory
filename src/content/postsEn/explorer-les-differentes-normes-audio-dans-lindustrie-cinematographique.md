The film industry safeguards the quality of its output, from production through post-production, through a set of audio standards. With demand for audio quality rising and production needs growing more diverse, understanding the different standards available has become essential. This post covers the film standards beyond the widely adopted EBU R128, including EBU R99 and CALM. These standards offer alternative guidance on ensuring a film's audio meets a given benchmark, as well as guidance on staying compliant with relevant legislation. Each has its own strengths and weaknesses, and this post will walk you through the key differences between them. We'll also look at how to use these alternative standards to build a top-tier sound for your film production.

## 1. EBU R128: A Standard for Loudness Control in Europe

When it comes to film standards, one European standard stands out: EBU R128. It's a loudness control standard widely used for dubbing in film and television. Created by the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) in 2010, it provides guidelines for loudness, ensuring consistent sound levels across every platform. That means whether you're watching a film in a theater, streaming it online, or watching it on TV, you'll always get clear, consistent sound. With EBU R128, sound engineers can easily calibrate audio levels to make sure viewers get the same experience no matter where they watch the film.

## 2. EBU R99: A Standard for Measuring Sound Levels

The second European standard, EBU R99, is designed specifically for measuring sound levels in theatrical films. This standard ensures a film's sound levels stay uniform across every cinema, even when it's been dubbed or mixed in different countries. It also sets limits on audio levels — such as maximum peak level, maximum loudness, and maximum true peak level — so that the peak loudness level in a cinema never exceeds 105 dB. The European Broadcasting Union created this standard to keep film sound levels consistent worldwide, so viewers get the same sound quality wherever they watch.

## 3. CALM: A US Broadcasting Regulation

Beyond that, the common film standards are the European ones: EBU R128 and EBU R99. EBU R128 is the most widely adopted film standard, partly because it sets an international benchmark for audio levels in film, television, and radio. It's also the standard most used for film dubbing in Europe. EBU R99 sees less use, but it governs audio levels in film and television and includes a number of requirements around audio compression and dynamic range. Finally, CALM is a US broadcasting regulation that caps the loudness of TV commercials, along with the sound levels of other types of programming.

## 4. Why These Standards Matter for Audio and Video Production

The European standard EBU R128 is a core building block of audio and video production. That's because EBU R128 lays out the technical prerequisites for film, cinema, and dubbing. It helps ensure audio levels stay consistent and high-quality, through the use of loudness, dynamic range, and audio range normalization. This standard also helps ensure sound is audible and that levels stay balanced and comfortable. What makes these standards important is that they help guarantee audio and video production reaches the best possible quality. EBU R99 and CALM are equally essential pieces of that puzzle, helping ensure audio and video production stays safe and comfortable for viewers.

## 5. Why Sound Engineers Should Know Them

As a sound engineer, it's important to know audio standards beyond the ones you use in the studio. In film, there are several other standards worth knowing, such as EBU R128, EBU R99, and CALM. These European standards are widely used across the film industry and are essential to ensuring films are properly dubbed and that audio levels are correct in the final production. Knowing these additional standards helps sound engineers understand the audio requirements for film and can lead to more precise mixing decisions.

## 6. Understanding the Key Differences Between the Standards

If you work in the film industry, you know there's a wide range of audio standards to comply with. While the European R128 standard is the most commonly used audio benchmark, other film standards deserve a closer look, such as EBU R99 and CALM. Understanding the key differences between these standards is essential to making sure your audio holds up when it comes to dubbing films. R128 focuses on loudness normalization, while EBU R99 and CALM both aim to keep audio levels uniform across a variety of media. That said, all three standards are essential to ensuring film audio reaches the highest possible quality.

## 7. How to Implement These Standards

Film standards have established the European R128 standard as the benchmark for properly calibrating loud sound. It covers both dubbing and theatrical sound, and it's built largely around the Loudness Range (LRA) and Integrated Loudness (I) parameters. To implement these standards, you need the right equipment, such as a loudness meter and specialized audio software. It's also essential to understand the different loudness ranges (short-term, momentary, integrated, and so on) and use them correctly. Finally, it's important to monitor audio during mixing and make sure it complies with R128 guidelines.

## 8. Benefits of Using These Standards in Audio and Video Production

The European R128 standard is well on its way to becoming the reference benchmark for audio and video production in film, television, and dubbing. It lays out a set of specifications for audio and video production, and using it brings several distinct advantages. For instance, R128 makes it possible to achieve higher audio levels with far less distortion. It also helps produce consistent sound and picture quality, meaning viewers can enjoy excellent audio and video quality no matter where they're watching. On top of that, R128 prevents excessive compression of audio and video dynamic range, resulting in a better overall viewing experience. Finally, using R128 helps ensure compliance with international standards and regulations for audio and video production.

In conclusion, while EBU R128, EBU R99, and CALM are all important to producing a quality film, each serves its own purpose and carries its own implications. EBU R128 is the current standard for sound and loudness levels, EBU R99 is designed to guide content producers on loudness levels, and CALM is the standard governing commercial advertising. Ultimately, understanding the nuances of each standard — and how to implement them correctly — is essential to producing the best possible film.
